What the instrument does

Four questions, asked of anything you're sure about.

  1. What else could explain this?

    The strongest rivals to your view, not weaker versions of it.

  2. What would we expect to see if that were true?

    Each explanation has to say what it would look like.

  3. Which observation would tell them apart?

    Most of what you've noticed fits every story. A little of it doesn't.

  4. Do you know that?

    Yes, no, or I don't know. Often the last one is the finding.

You might be right. You might change your mind. Or you might find there isn't enough evidence yet. The point isn't to hand you an answer. It is to make your confidence answerable to what you know.

Why it's different

We won't tell you what someone else thinks.

Ask a chatbot why someone did something and it will give you an answer. It can't know. Neither can we, so we don't pretend to. We help you find out what you'd need to know.

It will

  • Ask for what's missing
  • Set your read against its strongest rivals
  • Work out what each one predicts
  • Show you where your own answers disagree

It never will

  • Invent a motive
  • Invent an event
  • Claim to know another mind
  • Settle what reality hasn't

Understanding is not surrender. Certainty should be proportional to evidence.
