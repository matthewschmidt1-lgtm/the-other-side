An example

Say they agreed in the meeting, then did nothing.

The same behavior fits all four.

They disagreed and didn't say so
Agreement came fast, with no questions. They raise it later, with someone else.
They agreed and are overloaded
Other commitments are slipping too. They apologize when asked.
They thought it was someone else's job
They are surprised when you follow up.
They're waiting to see if you meant it
They move as soon as you ask a second time.

Your explanation may be right. But the observation is compatible with every one of these, so on its own it can't tell them apart.

Which of those have you actually seen?

Understanding is not surrender.

You don't have to agree with someone to see what they see.

Certainty should be proportional to evidence.

Experience makes confidence cheap. That is when it is most worth checking.

Before you trust a conclusion, know what would make it wrong.

Sometimes your first read is the best one. Then it will survive this.

Four questions, asked of anything you're sure about.

Confidence in your readAlmost certain · untested
ImpossibleCertain
  1. One of four

    What else could explain this?

    Not weaker versions of your view. The strongest rivals to it.

  2. Two of four

    What would we expect to see if that were true?

    An explanation that predicts nothing can't be tested. Each one has to say what it would look like.

  3. Three of four

    Which observation would tell them apart?

    Most of what you've noticed fits every explanation. A little of it doesn't. That little is the evidence.

  4. Four of four

    Do you know that?

    Often the fact that settles it is one you already have and hadn't used. Sometimes it is one question away.

We won't tell you what someone else thinks.

We'll help you find out what you'd need to know.

It will

  • Ask for what's missing
  • Set your read against its strongest rivals
  • Work out what each one predicts
  • Show you where your own answers disagree

It never will

  • Invent a motive
  • Invent an event
  • Claim to know another mind
  • Settle what reality hasn't

Four ways it ends. None of them ranked.

  • Your read held.

    By your account, it is still the best-supported explanation.

  • It held, lower than you started.

    Still leading. Not at the confidence you came in with.

  • Another explanation now leads.

    By your own second rating, not ours.

  • Not enough to know yet.

    Reality hasn't settled it. Now you know what would.
