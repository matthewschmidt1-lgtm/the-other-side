The Other Side Test it

You can be right and still not know why.

The Other Side is an instrument for testing what you're sure about.

Bring a conclusion about something that happened. It sets your explanation against its strongest rivals and shows whether what you've seen can tell them apart.

An example

Say they agreed in the meeting, then did nothing. What explains it?

The same behavior fits all four.

What you would expect to see if each were true

They disagreed and didn't say so
Agreement came fast, with no questions. They raise it later, with someone else.
They agreed and are overloaded
Other commitments are slipping too. They apologize when asked.
They thought it was someone else's job
They are surprised when you follow up.
They're waiting to see if you meant it
They move as soon as you ask a second time.

Your explanation may be right. But the observation is compatible with every one of these, so on its own it can't tell them apart.

That is the whole instrument: find what would tell them apart, then check whether you know it.

What you might leave with

Four ways it ends. None of them ranked.

  • Your read held.

    By your account, it is still the best-supported explanation.

  • It held, lower than you started.

    Still leading. Not at the confidence you came in with.

  • Another explanation now leads.

    By your own second rating, not ours.

  • Not enough to know yet.

    Reality hasn't settled it. Now you know what would.

It is not built to make you doubt yourself. Sometimes your first read is the best one, and then it survives this.

How a session goes

Four questions, asked of anything you're sure about.

An example. You say: “They ignored my message because they're angry with me.”

Your own rating of your readAlmost certainly · first rating
ImpossibleCertain
  1. One of four

    What else could explain this?

    • They're angry with you.
    • They're overwhelmed.
    • They didn't see it.
    • They don't know how to reply.
  2. Two of four

    What would we expect to see if that were true?

    Angry
    Short with you elsewhere too. Fine with others.
    Overwhelmed
    Slow with everyone.
    Didn't see it
    Replies at once when nudged.
    Unsure how to reply
    Reads it, starts, stops.
  3. Three of four

    Which observation would tell them apart?

    Not the silence. All four predict that. Whether they are slow with everyone does.

  4. Four of four

    Do you know that?

    You tap one: yes, no, or I don't know. “I don't know” is an answer here. Often it is the finding.

  5. Result

    Your read still leads. Lower than you started.

    You see what your answers fit, then rate everything again. Both ratings are yours. The instrument never assigns one.

What it won't do

We won't tell you what someone else thinks.

We'll help you find out what you'd need to know.

It will

  • Ask for what's missing
  • Set your read against its strongest rivals
  • Work out what each one predicts
  • Show you where your own answers disagree

It never will

  • Invent a motive
  • Invent an event
  • Claim to know another mind
  • Settle what reality hasn't

The name

The other side is not the other person's side.

It is every explanation you didn't choose. The question is never who is right. It is whether what you have seen can tell your explanation from the rest.

  • Understanding is not surrender.
  • Certainty should be proportional to evidence.
  • Before you trust a conclusion, know what would make it wrong.

Not a debate tool · Not advice · Not a test of who you are
